[ 01 ]The problem

Two problems that usually wear one coat.

When leads stop arriving, the question is never just "what is wrong with the funnel." It is also "can the reporting even say." A dashboard that renders is not the same as a dashboard that is connected to the outcome the business runs on. A conversion count of zero can mean no one converted, or that nothing was ever defined as a conversion.

These are different problems with different fixes. Spending on media to fix a measurement problem wastes the budget. Rebuilding measurement when the offer is the problem wastes the quarter. The audit separates them before recommending either.

[ 02 ]What we examine

The whole path, scoped to your business.

Not every engagement needs every item. Scope is set against your business model, the evidence available and the access you can grant.

01

Acquisition and traffic quality

Which channels bring people who behave like buyers, which bring volume that never engages, and what cannot be told apart yet.

02

Paid campaign structure

Objectives, optimisation events, placements, spend by ad set, and whether the platform was ever told what a lead is.

03

Landing pages, offers and forms

Every path a prospect can take, walked as a buyer. Field counts, friction, what fires on completion, where the path ends.

04

Calls to action and booking

Whether the highest-intent click can actually do what it promises, and where a request to book turns into a request to write.

05

Analytics instrumentation

Event definitions, key events, duplicate or missing tags, and whether any event anywhere means "a lead happened."

06

Attribution and reconciliation

Figures that appear in two systems, placed side by side. Agreement is confirmation; disagreement is a finding.

07

CRM, routing and follow-up

Where captured leads go, whether they arrive, who is told, and what the automation does and does not do after the first touch.

08

Reporting infrastructure

What the dashboards are connected to, under whose accounts, and which tabs describe the business versus decorate it.

09

Prioritised opportunities

What to fix first, what each fix requires, and what it is meant to settle. Ordered so each step establishes what the next depends on.

[ 04 ]How it works

Six steps, in order.

  1. 01

    Scope and access

    A conversation about what you sell, how you grow today and where it stalls. We agree the outcome that counts, the systems in scope and the read-only access you can grant.

  2. 02

    Read the reporting as given

    Dashboards, weekly reports, platform exports. We note what each asserts, what it declines to say, and which accounts it runs through.

  3. 03

    Go to the primary sources

    Reports describe; platforms show. We open the ad accounts, analytics, CRM and site that the reports are supposed to summarise, as far as access allows.

  4. 04

    Walk the funnel as a buyer

    Click what a prospect clicks. Read the forms. Watch the network. Record what fires, where each path ends and what a completion looks like to your systems.

  5. 05

    Reconcile and retest

    Figures that appear in two places are compared. Every conclusion, including our own first draft, is tested against the evidence and labelled with its confidence.

  6. 06

    Readout and plan

    Findings, a sequenced plan and the open questions, delivered as a private report and a working session. Then the first fixes, if you want us to make them.

[ 05 ]How findings are validated

Every claim carries its evidence and its confidence.

Confirmed

Seen directly in a primary source

A figure we read in the platform, a path we walked, a request we watched fire.

Supported

Consistent across sources

Two systems agree, or a report figure reconciles to the platform behind it.

Tentative

Plausible, not yet shown

One source, a client statement, or an inference. Labelled as such, with the test that would settle it.

Unknown

Outside the access we had

Stated plainly, with the access, approval or experiment required. We do not fill gaps with guesses.

We distinguish platform data, reporting outputs, client statements, direct observations and inferences, and we say which is which. When a second look overturns a first-pass conclusion, the report says so and explains why.

[ 07 ]Who it is for

Built for businesses where a conversation closes the deal.

The audit fits companies that sell through a pipeline: a form, a call, a proposal, a close. Professional services, training and coaching firms, B2B software and services, agencies, and any founder-led business where marketing is supposed to produce booked conversations and nobody can say with confidence whether it does.

It is not a fit for high-volume e-commerce measured purely on return on ad spend, or for teams that want a new dashboard rather than an answer to why the current one cannot explain performance. Scope and fee are agreed in writing before work starts, and the audit changes nothing in your accounts.
